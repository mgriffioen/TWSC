# Table Guide — Running This Weekend

## Characters

**Recommendation: build characters *before* the weekend** (a short video call or one-on-one chats in December). Friday night is precious, so don't spend 90 minutes on character creation with three new players.

- **Rules:** 5e (2014 or 2024; pick one and stick to it). Free D&D Beyond accounts for everyone; share characters into a D&D Beyond campaign so you can see all the sheets.
- **Everyone plays at level 3 for the whole weekend. Nobody levels up.** Level 3 is sturdy enough that new players won't die to one bad roll, and everyone already has their subclass, so the characters feel complete from the first scene. With no level-ups, new players learn one character sheet and keep it all weekend.
- **Level 3 means picking a subclass up front.** Do this together at Session Zero, not at the table. Steer new players to simple, fun options:
  - **Fighter (Champion)** or **Barbarian (Berserker)**: easy and satisfying. Great for Carl or Sean.
  - **Rogue (Thief)**: fun and sneaky, with clear options each turn.
  - **Cleric (Life)** or **Warlock (Fiend)**: for whoever wants magic without a huge spell list.
  - **Ranger (Hunter)**: a good pick if someone wants a bow and an animal-friendly vibe.
  - **Avoid for new players:** Wizard, Druid, Sorcerer (lots of spell bookkeeping).
- **Print a one-page cheat sheet per character** with their level 3 features highlighted. They'll use the same sheet all weekend.
- **Give a balanced party a nudge:** one tough front-liner, one healer, one sneak, one blaster.
- **Ask each player 3 questions** (and use their answers in the story):
  1. What's something your character is running from?
  2. Who's one person back home they'd do anything for?
  3. What would your character's perfect place to rest look like? (← **The Keeper offers them exactly this in Session 3.**)

## 🎁 Rewards Instead of Level-Ups

Without levels, the party gets stronger through **gear and boons** handed out at the two meal breaks. That keeps the sense of progress, and it's a good ritual at the real table.

### Morning Boons (Saturday breakfast, before Session 2)
Each player picks one, or rolls d6 for a surprise. Flavor each one with something their character did on Friday night.

| d6 | Boon | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A Healer's Satchel** | 2 *Potions of Healing* |
| 2 | **A Festival Prize Weapon** | Your weapon becomes +1 (or 20 pieces of +1 ammunition) |
| 3 | **The Winter King's Luck** | Once this weekend, turn one failed roll into a success |
| 4 | **A Warden's Cloak** | *Cloak of Protection* (+1 AC and saving throws; attunement) |
| 5 | **Auntie's Marked Card** | Once this weekend, swap your d20 roll with the DM's most recent d20 roll |
| 6 | **A Driftwood Charm** | Once per session, add 1d4 to a saving throw after seeing the roll |

### Gate Gifts (Saturday dinner, before Session 3)
Everyone gets both:
- **A Memory Token** 📜 (a real coin or trinket to hold). In Session 3, spend it to drop your Contentment by 2, or to gain advantage on one save against the Keeper. *It makes remembering something they can hold.*
- **One uncommon magic item** from this list (or the Undercroft loot they found): *Wand of Magic Missiles*, *Boots of Striding and Springing*, *Gauntlets of Ogre Power*, *Pearl of Power*, *Periapt of Wound Closure*, *Potion of Greater Healing* ×2, *Bag of Holding*.

Make sure the party also leaves Session 2 with **at least two doses of Emberoak Sap**. If they don't have it, the Gate Gifts include one vial, "found in a dead Gilded Hand scout's pack."

## ✉️ Dusty's Secret Envelope

Dusty is the experienced player. Give him a secret, sealed before the weekend:

> 📜 *Your character doesn't remember the last three years. You woke on the frozen riverbank outside Millhaven in a white robe with a gold spiral stitched on it, an empty glass vial in your fist, your mouth burning like you'd swallowed a coal. Sometimes you dream of a hill, a row of cabins, warm water, and a kind old man who never stops smiling. A line of verse comes to you at odd moments, though you don't know where you learned it: "I hear it in the deep heart's core." You have never told anyone. Don't tell the others — yet.*

- Pass him notes (or texts) with flashbacks at key moments: the Returned recognize him (S1), his handwriting in the Welcome Hall ledger and his reflection at the Glass Mere (S2), the Keeper's *"Your old lodge is just as you left it"* (S3).
- 🔀 In the finale he **remembers how he escaped**: he poured burning sap into the hot spring's drain. He decides when to tell the others. (If the party is floundering, slip him a note: "You remember now.")
- Ask Dusty to quietly be your **co-pilot** as well: help the new players with their sheets, model good play, and don't steamroll. Experienced players love having a job.

## 📱 Erik on FaceTime

Remote play works if you plan for it.

**Setup**
- A **dedicated device** (an iPad on a stand) for Erik, placed at the head of the table so he sees faces and the map, not one person's ear.
- A **second camera** (a phone on a gooseneck or overhead mount) pointed at the map and minis.
- A **Bluetooth speaker** for his audio so everyone hears him.
- **Digital dice:** D&D Beyond's dice roller shares rolls into the campaign log. Or let him roll on camera.
- A **shared group text** for handouts: send him a photo of every 📜 prop as you hand it out.
- Mail him his **golden invitation** ahead of time.

**Making him central, not peripheral**
- 🔮 **Give Erik's character a special role: The Seer.** His character has occasional visions. You **text him secret info** during play (a hidden door, a lie an NPC is telling, a puzzle clue). He chooses when and how to share it. This makes the remote player *the most informed person at the table*.
- 🔮 **The Seer's Floor** (S2, room 9) is built for him: he alone sees the safe runes and whispers one rune name per turn. Test sending him a photo mid-game during your tech check.
- 🌅 In S3, text him first when the sunset loops: *"The same bird just crossed the sun again."*
- **Call on him by name** every round in combat and every scene in roleplay. Remote players get talked over, so be deliberate.
- Keep the **initiative order on a card or whiteboard** in his camera view.
- Break every ~75 minutes. Screen fatigue is real.

## 🧭 Teaching New Players (Sean, Carl, Erik)

- **Explain only what's needed, when it's needed.** Teach ability checks in the tavern, combat in the brawl, spells when someone casts one.
- **Ask "What do you do?"**, not "Roll Perception." Let them describe; you pick the roll.
- **Cheat sheets:** give each new player a one-page card: *On your turn: Move + Action + (Bonus Action). Actions: Attack, Cast, Dash, Dodge, Help, Hide.* Highlight their two or three best moves.
- **Spotlight rotation:** after each scene, mentally check who hasn't had a moment.
- **Inspiration tokens:** give out a physical token for good roleplay or a great moment. It can be spent for advantage.
- **Fail forward.** A failed roll changes the situation; it doesn't stop the story.

## 🧰 DM Tools & Devices Checklist

**At the table**
- [ ] DM screen (or a folder with the session outline and tables printed)
- [ ] Index cards, one per major NPC (name, voice, catchphrase), placed face-up when introduced
- [ ] Whiteboard or big paper for the Undercroft node map (reveal as they go)
- [ ] Battle map/grid with minis or tokens (or theater-of-the-mind for small fights)
- [ ] Initiative tracker, visible to Erik's camera
- [ ] Extra dice sets for the new players (let them keep them as a souvenir)
- [ ] Inspiration tokens
- [ ] **Contentment trackers** for Session 3 (a d6 per player set face-up, or a card with 0–5 boxes)
- [ ] Timer (for puzzle hints and the Gate closing)

**Props 📜**
- [ ] Golden invitations (gold cardstock, wax seal, spiral stamp), one per player + mail Erik's
- [ ] Dusty's sealed envelope
- [ ] The Mayor's letter to the Gilded Hand Captain
- [ ] Two halves of the Undercroft map (tear it!)
- [ ] Hall of Doors handout (the five sigils)
- [ ] Seer's Floor: 25 rune cards + Erik's key (photo)
- [ ] Seven **lodge keys** with carved or drawn sigils (pointing hand, spiral, eye, wave, heart, sun, hound)
- [ ] Small glass vials with red liquid for **Emberoak Sap**
- [ ] A rubber fish or a tiny treasure chest for the baby mimic
- [ ] The troll's toll ledger (a real notebook: write down the secrets players tell!)

**Atmosphere**
- [ ] Playlists: *festival and tavern* (S1, with a bouncy offbeat jig or two), *cave ambience* (S2), and *summer* for S3. For the reveal, play a song slowed to 50% speed; it's deeply creepy.
- [ ] Lighting: lamps for the city, low light or candles for the cave, bright and warm for the vale… then cut the lights at the reveal 💥
- [ ] Themed food: festival fare Friday (fried fish, skewers, flatbreads); **ribs and corn Saturday night** for Session 3. Watch the players hesitate once the Keeper serves the feast in-game.

**Apps**
- D&D Beyond (sheets, rules, dice log, shared campaign)
- A soundboard app (Syrinscape, Tabletop Audio, or a Spotify playlist)
- Owlbear Rodeo (a free web map) if Erik needs to see the map clearly

## Safety & Comfort (light-touch)
The adventure uses real memories. Jokes everyone laughs *with* are great; anything that might sting someone at the table, skip it. A quick "anything off-limits?" at the start of Friday is enough.
