# Story Bible — The Truth Behind the Curtain

*DM eyes only. This is what's REALLY going on. Everything else in the folder hangs off this.*

## The pitch (what the players think)

Every generation, golden invitations stamped with a **perfect spiral** appear to a chosen few: an invitation to **Shangri-La**, a legendary hidden vale where it is always summer and no one grows old or sad. The way there lies somewhere beneath the harbor city of **Millhaven**.

This year, the party receives invitations. 📜

Millhaven is locked in ice in the dead of winter, in the middle of its **Winter Festival**, the week the harbor ice is ritually broken. But people who went seeking Shangri-La have been vanishing, and the city has been strangely, suspiciously lucky for three hundred years.

## The truth (what's actually happening)

**Shangri-La is alive.** It is an ancient, colossal **mimic**, a creature the size of a valley. It has grown so old and clever that it imitates not a chest or a door but an entire *paradise*. It reads its guests and becomes **the place where they were happiest**.

For this party, it becomes a lakeside vale: seven timber lodges on a grassy hill, a raft out on the water with a long yellow chute, a feast over the fire, music drifting from somewhere, and a kindly old keeper puttering around on a little golden cart.

- The groundskeepers are pseudopod puppets, all wearing the same face.
- The hot springs are digestive pools.
- The feast is bait made of its own flesh.
- The lake is its stomach. The yellow chute is its tongue.
- It doesn't kill guests quickly. It feeds on their **contentment**, slowly, for decades. Guests forget their names, their homes, their friends.

It speaks through a puppet: **Oldmin Paulus, the Keeper**, a weathered, friendly groundskeeper who knows everyone's name and is so glad they finally came.

**Its heart lies beneath the Hound's Hearth**, the one lodge no one is allowed to stay in.

### Its weakness: Emberoak Sap
The Host has devoured everything for a thousand years. The one thing its gut can't bear is **fire from within**. Long ago, a people who lived in the Undercroft learned this. They tapped three great petrified oaks for a searing red resin, **Emberoak Sap**, and used it to drive the creature back. Their carvings at the Three Oaks show figures pouring red liquid into a vast mouth, and the mouth recoiling.

Sources of Emberoak Sap:
1. **The Returned** (Session 1): their leader keeps one nearly empty vial. *"It's how I got out."* One use.
2. **The Three Oaks steam hall** (Session 2): the main supply, if the party solves or survives it.
3. **Dusty's character** woke from his lost years with an empty vial and a burning mouth (see his envelope).

### Where it came from: Mother
Deep in the Undercroft is the fossil of an even larger mimic, with a crude grave marker before it: **HERE LIES MOTHER.** Fresh flowers lie on the stone. The Host is her child. It was alone in the dark for centuries, and it learned that if it becomes the place people love most, they never leave. It isn't only hungry. It's *lonely.* (Use this if you want a sympathetic ending.)

## The deal with Millhaven

Three hundred years ago, the city's founders struck a bargain. The Host gives Millhaven luck: the harbor ice always breaks on time, the fishing never fails, the plague passes it by. In return, the city sends it *guests*. The golden invitations are sent by the city's **Lantern Council**, whose current head, **Mayor Corwin Vell**, is the party's quest-giver.

## Themes

- **Remembering vs. forgetting.** The Host's power is forgetting. The party's power is *remembering*: telling stories of home and of each other breaks the spell.
- **Too good to be true.** Every gift has a hook in it.
- **The perfect memory as a trap.** Paradise looks like the best summer you ever had. The fight is about choosing your real companions over the perfect memory of them.
- **Winter outside, summer inside.** A frozen festival city above, an endless summer below.

## 💥 Twist Menu — pick 2–3, save the rest

**Chosen: ________**

1. **Paradise is the monster.** *(Core; recommended.)* Mid-Session 3 the lake drains, the yellow chute uncurls into a tongue, and the lodges lean inward like teeth.
2. **The quest-giver is in on it.** Mayor Vell sent the invitations. Reveal it through a letter found in the Undercroft, or have the Mayor appear in the vale, lounging by the water.
3. **Dusty's character has been here before.** (See his secret envelope in [09_Table_Guide.md](09_Table_Guide.md).) The Keeper greets him: *"Welcome back. Your old lodge is just as you left it."*
4. **The missing person doesn't want to be rescued.** Tobias Vell is blissfully happy and fights to stay.
5. **The rival captain is a former guest.** Captain Saye escaped years ago and is desperate to get *back in*.
6. **Time ran differently.** In the epilogue, roll 1d10 in front of them for the years that have passed outside. Or it was five minutes.
7. **The Host offers a deal.** Leave one behind and the rest go free.
8. **The Host is lonely, not evil.** If the party offers it something besides guests (a promise to return, a companion, its mother's memory), it lets everyone go. A peaceful ending is possible.
9. **Sequel hook.** A smaller invitation arrives: "under new management." The baby mimic they spared in Session 1 has grown up and opened a place of its own.

## Continuity notes

- Shangri-La's entrance is the **Golden Gate** at the bottom of the Undercroft. Over it, an old inscription the Cartographer translates as *"Rest here, and be content."* Scratched inside it, by someone who escaped: *"People forget. But they shouldn't."*
- The invitations are thin gold leaf, actually a **shed scale of the Host**, stamped with the perfect spiral. *Detect magic* shows transmutation and enchantment. Burned, they smell like roasting meat.
- The Host cannot harm guests who are *content*. Anger, fear and homesickness make guests "indigestible," and dangerous to it.
- The Returned can't stand the smell of roasting meat, which is why they live on Ham Point among the smokehouses: a penance, and a guard against forgetting.
- Shangri-La copies memories imperfectly: carvings change when you aren't looking, the sunset never quite finishes setting, and the Keeper's cart leaves no tracks.

## A few personal touches (kept on purpose)

The adventure is a classic D&D story. A handful of details quietly echo the group's own summer weekends. Play them straight and never point them out:
- **Oldmin Paulus, the Keeper**, and his little golden cart.
- **The vale itself**: a lake below a hill of seven lodges, and a raft with a long yellow chute.
- **The Hound's Hearth**, the lodge nobody stays in.
- **Ham Point**, the smokehouse headland.
- **Millhaven**, **The Two Decks** and **Fisherman's Luck**.
- **"People forget. But they shouldn't."** (Sean's line), scratched inside the Golden Gate.
- **"I hear it in the deep heart's core"** over Dusty's character's lodge.
