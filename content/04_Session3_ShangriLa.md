# Session 3 — Shangri-La (Saturday Evening)

**Length:** 3–4 hours  **Level:** 3  **Goal:** Wonder → creeping unease → dread → 💥 REVEAL → the Heart beneath the Hound's Hearth → escape across the lake → epilogue.

The session has three movements. Let the first one be *genuinely nice*. The contrast makes the reveal land.

---

## Movement 1 — The Vale (≈60 min)

**Read-aloud:**
> It's summer. Somehow, beneath a frozen city, it's high summer. A grassy hill rolls down to a small, sparkling lake. Seven timber lodges stand in a row along the ridge, each with a sigil carved over its door. A cookfire smokes at the bottom of the hill. Somewhere, a band is playing a bright, bouncing tune. Out on the water a wooden raft bobs, and from it a long chute of polished yellow amber curls down into the lake. Guests in loose summer clothes laugh and splash. Everyone is smiling.

### Too perfect
The vale is built from the guests' happiest memories, so it fits each character a little too well. Over the first half hour, let each PC find one detail that matches their own answer to *"What would your character's perfect place to rest look like?"* ([09_Table_Guide.md](09_Table_Guide.md)): a favorite food on the feast table, a song from their childhood, a view exactly like the one from home. At first it's delightful. By the third detail, it should feel like the place is *listening.*

### Oldmin Paulus, the Keeper (the Host)
A weathered, sun-browned old groundskeeper in a broad hat who rides a little golden cart that trundles along without a horse. He welcomes everyone by name. He knows the answers they gave in Session 1 about *why they accepted the invitation*. He's folksy, generous and funny, and he drives everyone to their lodge. The cart leaves no tracks.

### 🏡 The Seven Lodges
Each guest is given a lodge key carved with a sigil. Each lodge has its own temptation: using it grants its benefit **and +1 Contentment.**

| Sigil over the door | Inside | Benefit | The tell |
|---|---|---|---|
| **An open book** | A reading nook with every book you ever loved | Advantage on one INT check | Past the middle, every book's pages are blank. |
| **A perfect spiral** | A yard where every thrown thing flies true | +1 to ranged attacks for the session | The spiral matches the invitations, and the Mayor's ring |
| **An open, watchful eye** | A bunk room of old cloaks, masks and a **crate of sky-rockets** | 10 temp HP | The crate is stenciled *"Confiscated from guests."* |
| **A wave beneath a roof** | A deck with a steaming hot spring | Remove all conditions and exhaustion | The water is a little *too* warm, and slightly thick |
| **A heart, with verse carved beneath** | *Dusty's character's lodge.* The lintel reads: *"I hear it in the deep heart's core."* | Advantage on WIS saves | His own initials, carved into the bedpost years ago |
| **A sun that never sets** | A porch facing a perfect sunset | Regain all spell slots | The sunset never finishes setting. The same bird crosses it every minute. (Text Erik first.) |
| **A hound** (the Hound's Hearth) | **Locked.** The Keeper: *"Oh, nobody stays in that one."* | — | It's humming. The floor is warm. The Heart is beneath it. |

### Pleasures of the vale (each = +1 Contentment and a small benefit; see **Shangri-La Pleasures** in the roll tables)
- **The Feast:** roasts over the fire, fresh bread, honeyed fruit. Heals to full. (The players may be wary of eating, which is perfect.)
- **The Driftwood Hut**, a little bar on the shore, serving cool cordials that never run dry.
- **Lawn games with the Keeper:** bowls on the green. Win and the Keeper owes you a favor (spend it to delay the reveal or ask one true question). Lose and gain +1 extra Contentment.
- **The Raft:** swim out and slide down the yellow chute. Pure joy. Gain Inspiration… and +2 Contentment.
- **The Bonfire Leap** at dusk. Acrobatics DC 12 for glory.
- **Sky-rockets** over the lake at dusk. Everybody's happy. Everybody. Forever.

### ⭐ The Contentment Track (core mechanic)
Track each PC's Contentment on a card in front of them (0–5). Players can SEE it, but you don't explain what it means.
- **+1** for each pleasure, lodge comfort, food, drink or gift accepted.
- At **3**: disadvantage on WIS saves against the Keeper's charms. They start forgetting small things (*"What was the name of the city you came from? …Hm."*).
- At **5**: the character *doesn't want to leave*. The player must succeed on a DC 15 WIS save to take any action against Shangri-La.
- **−1** whenever a character is hurt, scared, angry or homesick, or tastes **Emberoak Sap** (−2, and 1d4 fire damage to yourself).
- If the party completed the Glass Mere perfectly, everyone starts at 0 and is "anchored."
- **Memory Tokens** (the Gate Gifts handed out at dinner): spend one to drop Contentment by 2, or to gain advantage on one save against the Keeper.

### Finding the Missing
The party finds **Tobias Vell** lounging in a hot spring with a drink, deliriously happy, his skin a little… translucent. He's been here "a long weekend" (actually three weeks; some guests have been here eighty years).
- 🔀 *(Saved twist #4, not chosen; skip by default.)* He refuses to leave and argues convincingly. 🎲 Persuasion DC 15, or roleplay it out. Reminding him of home gives advantage.
- Auntie Grell's sister, Mira, is here too: twenty years old, sixty years after she arrived.

### Clues that something's wrong (drop 1–2 every 10 minutes)
- The Keeper has no shadow. His cart leaves no tracks.
- The books in the open-book lodge go blank halfway through.
- The sunset never finishes setting.
- The groundskeepers all have the same face. Exactly the same.
- A guest's hand has partly grown into the arm of his lawn chair.
- The ice in the drinks never melts.
- The baby mimic (if it's with the party) is terrified and hides in someone's pack.
- 💥 **Twist #3 (always use it):** the Keeper greets **Dusty's** character: *"Welcome back. Your old lodge is just as you left it."*

## Movement 2 — 💥 The Reveal (≈20 min)

Triggers when someone forces the Hound's Hearth, tries to leave with the missing person, uses Emberoak Sap, or 60 minutes have passed.

> The music slows… and slows… until the bright tune is a long, low groan. Every guest, every groundskeeper, every smiling face turns toward you at once. The Keeper sighs and takes off his hat. "Leaving early? Ah, that's a shame." Then the hill beneath you **breathes.** The lake drains in a great gurgling swirl. The yellow chute on the raft *uncurls*, wet and pink, and tastes the air. The seven lodges creak and lean inward like teeth. You are not in a valley. You are in a *mouth.*

💥 **Twist #2: the Mayor is here.** As the lake drains, the party spots **Mayor Vell** on a lounger at the water's edge in a white robe with a gold spiral, a drink in his hand. He came down by his own road to "renew the bargain" and deliver them in person. Play him tragic (horrified: *"It was supposed to be painless!"*) or slimy (*"Nothing personal, friends. Millhaven needs its luck."*), whichever you picked in [05_NPCs.md](05_NPCs.md). In the fight, **The Bargain** protects him from the Heart; he flees for the Gate when it starts to go badly. If the party catches him, his ledger and signet ring set up the epilogue.

💥 Saved twists (not chosen; use only if the table needs a jolt):
- **Twist #5:** Captain Saye is in a hot spring, half-dissolved and smiling: *"I got out once. I came back. You will too."*
- **Twist #7:** The Keeper offers a deal: *"Leave one behind, and the rest of you can go. I'll even let you take the boy."*
- **Twist #8:** The Keeper, quietly: *"Mother always said a place is only a home if somebody stays."*

## Movement 3 — The Heart & the Escape (≈90 min)

### Phase A — Fight through the staff
The Host's puppets attack: **groundskeepers**, **feast mimics**, **towel golems** and **pool horrors** ([06_Monsters.md](06_Monsters.md)). The terrain shifts every round (🎲 **Lair Actions**, below).
- 🔥 **The sky-rocket crate** from the watchful-eye lodge can be lit (an action) and aimed: 4d6 fire in a 10-ft area, DEX DC 13 for half. Each PC gets one use.

### Phase B — The Heart beneath the Hound's Hearth (Boss)
Break into the Hound's Hearth. The floor is gone. Below, in a pit of meat-scented steam, is the **Heart**: a massive, pulsing, golden organ, with the Keeper's body attached to it by a long glistening cord. Stat block in [06_Monsters.md](06_Monsters.md).

**⭐ The Remembering**
Once per round, as a bonus action, a player can **tell a short memory of home, or of another character**: a backstory moment, something that happened earlier this weekend's adventure, or (if they like) a real story about someone at the table. When they do:
- the target's Contentment drops by 1, **and**
- the next attack against the Heart deals **+1d8 psychic damage** ("It can't digest a memory it didn't make").

Introduce it in-world: the first time someone resists or speaks of home, the Heart *flinches*. Let the players figure out the rest.

**🔥 Emberoak Sap**
- **Hurling a vial into the Heart's open maw** takes an action and a ranged attack (or an Athletics check if they get close). On a hit it deals **4d10 fire damage**, and the Heart is **stunned until the end of its next turn.**
- **Coating weapons** in sap (a bonus action) adds +1d6 fire damage for 1 minute.
- Dusty's character's buried memory: he *remembers* how he escaped last time. He poured the sap into the hot spring's drain. He decides when to share it.

### Lair Actions (initiative 20) 🎲 d6
1. **The Feast Hungers:** tables lash out with tablecloth tongues. DC 13 DEX or restrained.
2. **The Springs Overflow:** a 10-ft area floods with digestive water. 2d6 acid, DC 13 CON for half.
3. **The Song Slows:** everyone makes a DC 13 WIS save or gains +1 Contentment.
4. **The Chute:** the yellow tongue lashes 30 ft from the lakebed. One creature: DC 13 DEX or pulled 20 ft toward the Heart.
5. **The Hill Tilts:** everyone slides 10 ft toward the Heart.
6. **A Gift:** the Keeper offers one PC something mid-combat. Accepting heals 2d8… and +2 Contentment.

### Phase C — The Escape (Chase)
When the Heart drops, the vale convulses, gags, and starts to **spit everything out.** The lake comes roaring back as a flood. The party must cross it to the Golden Gate, carrying any rescued guests, on the **air-bladder raft** from the boathouse (a raft of stitched, inflated hides) or the bullywug barge if they brought it.
- Run it as a **chase**: 3 rounds. Each round, each player describes how they get past an obstacle and rolls a check of their choice (DC 12). 🎲 Roll **Escape Complications** ([08_Roll_Tables.md](08_Roll_Tables.md)) each round.
- The Gate is closing. If anyone is still at Contentment 5, a friend must go back for them. **That's the climactic moment.**

## Epilogue

They burst out into the Undercroft and climb up into Millhaven just as the festival ends. The harbor ice, broken by the hammer, is drifting out to sea. For the first time in three hundred years, the city will have to make its own luck.
- What happens to Mayor Vell and the Lantern Council? They saw him in the vale, so this is the big question. (Justice, a mob, a trial, or the Mayor flees.)
- Millhaven's luck starts to run out. Did they doom the city or free it?
- Auntie Grell meets her sister again, if they brought Mira home.
- Each player gets a closing moment: *"What does your character do the next morning?"*
- 🔀 If they took the peaceful route (saved Twist #8, only if you used it), the vale might still send invitations: just a long weekend, once a year.

**Final stinger** (pick one):
- **Twist #6:** roll 1d10 in front of everyone. That's how many years have passed outside.
- **Twist #9:** at breakfast, a new invitation slides under the door of The Two Decks. Smaller card. Cheaper gilt. *"A more intimate retreat. Under new management."* A tiny spiral, slightly wobbly, as if drawn by someone young. → **Sequel hook for next year.**
