# Puzzles

**Design rules for this group:**
1. **No single solution.** Every puzzle has several ways through. Reward creativity over "the right answer."
2. **Failure is interesting, not a wall.** Wrong answers cost something (HP, gold, Contentment, embarrassment) and the story keeps moving.
3. **Hints on a timer.** ⏱️ If they're stuck for 5 minutes, an NPC or the environment gives a hint. At 10 minutes, give a bigger one. Never let a puzzle stall the night.

| Puzzle | Session / room | Who shines |
|---|---|---|
| The Steam Hall | S2 room 4 | Problem-solvers |
| The Toll | S2 room 5 | Everyone (jokes, secrets, songs) |

## 1. The Steam Hall (Session 2, room 4 — The Three Oaks)

Three petrified oaks surround an ancient stone steam hall. Inside are a sealed chest and three oak roots set into the floor like levers. It's **too hot**, and it gets hotter.

**The heat:** At the start of each round inside, everyone makes a CON save, DC 10 + the number of rounds they've been inside. On a failure they take 1d6 fire damage. On a failure by 5 or more they also gain 1 level of exhaustion.

**The levers:** The three root-levers cool the hall if pulled in the right order. A clue is carved over the door: *"The youngest first. The eldest last. Then breathe."* Each oak outside has a clean, polished cut showing its rings: **Left Oak: 7 rings. Door Oak: 12 rings. Right Oak: 3 rings.** The correct order is **Right, Left, Door** (3 → 7 → 12).

*On the map* (Rm 04; the party enters from the bottom, so their left is the map's left): the **Left Oak** and **Right Oak** stand behind the hall at its back corners, and the **Door Oak** stands just in front of the entrance steps. Inside, the **left lever** and **right lever** are the roots reaching in from the Left and Right Oaks. The **back lever**, which comes through the rear wall, belongs to the Door Oak: its root runs under the hall and surfaces at the back. Players can trace each root with Investigation or Survival DC 10, or just by looking. The stump tops on the map show dozens of rings, so give the 7 / 12 / 3 counts by description.

| Approach | Outcome |
|---|---|
| Solve the lever order | ✅ The steam stops, the hall cools, and the chest opens: **Emberoak Sap** ×3 + the **Oakheart Mace**. Bonus: the hall becomes a perfect short-rest spot. |
| Wrong order | ⚠️ A steam blast: 2d6 fire in the room (DEX DC 12 half), then they can try again. |
| **Cool it creatively** (harbor ice, a frozen fish from the festival, *Shape Water*, *Ray of Frost*) | ✅ Buys 2 free rounds per idea. Reward it loudly. |
| Tough it out and grab the chest | ⚠️ Works, but expect HP loss and maybe exhaustion. The chest is heavy (Athletics DC 12 to drag it out). |
| Leave | ❌ No mace, no sap. They'll have only the Returned's single vial. |

## 2. The Toll (Troll Bridge — Session 2, room 5)

Old Mossback sits under the bridge with a ledger and a rubber stamp. *"Toll's one each. Rules is rules."* Each person must pay **one toll**. He accepts only these:

| Toll | What the PLAYER does | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **A Joke** | Tells a real joke. 🎲 The troll rolls d20 and laughs on 10+. (Rig it: if the table laughs, he laughs at the table laughing.) | ✅ Cross. A great joke earns a **Troll Token** (call him once for help). |
| **A Secret** | Tells a true (harmless) secret about their character. | ✅ Cross. The troll writes it in his ledger, and 💥 the Keeper *quotes it* in Session 3. |
| **A Song** | Sings a verse. Anything. | ✅ Cross. Bad singing makes the troll weep and give a **Pebble of Pity** (+1 to one roll). |
| **A True Tale** | Tells a short story from their character's past. | ✅ Cross. The troll stamps it "APPROVED" and warns them about the kobolds. |
| **Gold** | 25 gp | ✅ Cross. Boring. The troll sighs. |
| **Refuse / sneak / fight** | — | ❌ The troll (*Troll* stat block, CR 5, too strong; make clear this is a terrible idea) shakes the bridge: DC 13 DEX or fall into the river (2d6, and you wash up at the Fishfolk Camp). |

🔀 *Clever option:* convince the troll the Gilded Hand never paid their toll, and he becomes an ally against them (or against the kobolds).

## 3. The Glass Mere (Session 2, room 7)

A still lake ringed with pale sand. The only way across is a stone path that's invisible except in reflections. The reflections show the party, but in **memories.**

**How it works:** Each character looks in and sees a memory from their past. **The player describes it**: where they were, who was with them, what they lost or loved. When they do, a section of the path lights up.
- New players: prompt with a sensory detail. *"Carl, in the water you see a summer night, a fire, and someone you haven't seen in years. Who is it?"*
- Erik (the Seer): his reflection appears on the *underside* of the lake, so he sees things the others can't. Pass him a note with a secret about the hidden path.

| Result | Outcome |
|---|---|
| Everyone shares | ✅ The path fully lights. Everyone gets **Inspiration**, and Contentment starts at 0 and "anchored" in Session 3. |
| Some share | ⚠️ A partial path. The rest must swim or take the barge (DC 12 Athletics); reflections grab at them (DC 12 WIS or 1d6 psychic). |
| Take the barge without sharing | ⚠️ A safe crossing, but the reflections watch silently. No Inspiration. |
| Cross with nothing | ❌ The reflections climb out of the lake as **Mirror Doubles** of each PC (*Shadow* stat block). Defeating your double means you remember why you came. |

💥 **Dusty's reflection** wears a white robe with a gold spiral and holds a lodge key. Pass him a note.

## 4. The Hall of Five Doors (Session 2, room 8)

Five doors in a circular hall, each carved with a sigil. Above them: **"FOUR DOORS TELL THE TRUTH. ONE DOOR LEADS THROUGH."** Each door has one inscription:

| Door | Sigil | Inscription |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | A coiled serpent | "The way is Door II." |
| **II** | A tongue of flame | "I am not the way." |
| **III** | A lit lantern | "Door I is lying." |
| **IV** | A snowflake | "The way is an odd-numbered door." |
| **V** | A ship | "The way is neither Door I nor Door V." |

**Solution: Door III (the lantern).** Door III says Door I is lying, so exactly one of I and III is the liar, which means II, IV and V are all telling the truth. II rules out Door 2, IV leaves 1, 3 or 5, and V rules out 1 and 5, leaving **Door III**. Door I is the liar. Check: if III were the liar instead, I would be truthful and the way would be Door II, but II says it isn't. That contradiction makes the answer unique.

📜 *Prop idea:* print this as a handout. The Cartographers' Guild clue (Session 1) gives the hint: *"Only one door lies. Find the liar and you've found the way."*

**Wrong doors.** Each opens onto a small chamber, then a hallway loops back to the Hall:

| Door | What's behind it |
|---|---|
| I — Serpent | A pit of harmless garter snakes and a dead adventurer's journal: *"Not the serpent. The light."* (A hint.) |
| II — Flame | A banquet table, steaming with food. Delicious. Then the platter is a *Mimic*. +1 Contentment if you ate before the fight. |
| IV — Snowflake | A frozen chamber with a bubbling hot spring. A short rest is possible (roll Cave Weirdness). The spring is warm… too warm. Get out. |
| V — Ship | A dry dock with a rotting boat. Then the room **floods**: DC 12 Athletics or 1 level of exhaustion. Floating on the water: a **Potion of Healing**. |

## 5. The Seer's Floor (Session 2, room 9 — Erik's puzzle)

The Welcome Hall floor is a 5×5 grid of stone tiles, each carved with a rune. The counter is across the room. **Only the Seer can see which tiles are safe**: in his visions, they glow. They're the Host's blind spots, the things it doesn't have and isn't watching, and he's seeing through its eyes.

**Setup:** 📜 Use the battle map (*Rm 09 - The Welcome Hall & the Seer's Floor*): the runes are carved right into the tiles. **Hand Erik the printed key card** ([handouts/04-Seer's Floor key (Erik only).png](handouts/04-Seer's%20Floor%20key%20(Erik%20only).png)). The party must cross from the entrance row to the counter row stepping only orthogonally (no diagonals). The rule: **Erik may only whisper one rune name per turn.** The party chooses where to step.

**The grid** as drawn on the map (row 1 is beside the counter; row 5 is by the entrance doors)

| | A | B | C | D | E |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Star | Moon | Mountain | Pine | Spiral |
| **2** | Waves | Flame | Anvil | Hammers | Scales |
| **3** | Wheat | Eye | Sun | Hourglass | Key |
| **4** | Knot (triquetra) | Tower | Lyre | Stag | Compass |
| **5** | Book | Feather | Knot (square) | Horseshoe | Crystals |

*Two knots:* call the row 4 one the **Triple Knot** and the row 5 one the **Square Knot** so Erik's whispers are never ambiguous.

**Key (Erik only):**
- 🟦 **Safe path (6):** HORSESHOE → STAG → HOURGLASS → HAMMERS → ANVIL → MOUNTAIN *(D5 → D4 → D3 → D2 → C2 → C1)*
- 🟥 **Traps:** BOOK, WHEAT, EYE, SUN, WAVES. Each deals 1d6 damage (steam or acid) and a groundskeeper (*Thug*) appears.
- ⬛ **The worst tile:** SPIRAL, the Keeper's own mark. The floor tips the stepper down a chute into **the Luggage** mimic, and the Steward is alerted (no peaceful check-in).
- ⬜ **Everything else:** the tile cracks under you. Nothing happens, but you step back and lose your turn.

**The logic (for you, or for a sharp player who asks):** the safe runes are things **Shangri-La doesn't have**: the road away (horseshoe), the wild (stag), time passing (hourglass), honest work (hammers, anvil), and the cold mountains of home. The traps are the vale's lures: the open book, the eye, the sun and the waves are four of the seven **lodge sigils** (players will recognize them in Session 3), and the wheat is the feast. The **Key** tile is a deliberate temptation: it's harmless, but stepping up the right-hand column from it leads through Scales to the Spiral.

| Result | Outcome |
|---|---|
| Clean crossing | ✅ The bell rings by itself: *"Honored guests!"* Everyone starts Session 3 with a free pleasure. |
| Crossing with some traps | ✅ A normal check-in. |
| The Spiral tile | ❌ Luggage fight and Steward fight together. |

⏱️ Five minutes max. After that, the safe tiles start glowing for everyone.

## 6. Leaving the Vale (Session 3, the final "puzzle")

To pass back through the Golden Gate, each character must settle up. The Keeper's voice comes from everywhere: *"Leaving so soon? Well, let's settle what you took."*

The bill is everything they accepted in the vale. Each pleasure or gift must be **given back** (lost), or paid for with **1d6 HP per item**. 🔀 *Creative clause:* they can refuse to settle at all and **smash the Gate** (STR DC 18, and the group can help), which collapses the Undercroft behind them and changes Millhaven forever.

## 7. The Scales of Regret *(spare: drop it anywhere)*

An altar with a bronze scale. One pan holds a stone labeled **"WHAT YOU CARRY."** The empty pan is labeled **"WHAT YOU LET GO."** The scale weighs **meaning**, not weight: a sentimental item plus a sentence about why it matters, or a confessed regret from a character's past, moves it a lot.

| Result | Outcome |
|---|---|
| Balanced with a meaningful sacrifice | ✅ Reward; the item **returns in Session 3** (the Keeper had it all along). |
| Balanced by cheating | ⚠️ Reward, but the room floods. |
| Overloaded | ⚠️ Reward and item lost, but a shortcut opens. |
| Give up | ❌ The "What You Carry" stone follows them, rolling sadly, for the rest of the dungeon. |
